      I left with little more than the clothes on my back and a heel of bread wrapped in cloth.
No one stopped me. No one spoke. They only watched as I walked beyond the fields and the last stone walls.

The wilds do not welcome kindly.
I had not gone far when men waylaid me. Stripped me of my food, my tools, and near all my garments, leaving me with little to guard against the cold.
Left for dead, if they had their way.

The land is cruel.
The rains fall heavy and sudden, the nights colder than iron. Hunger gnaws sharp, and beasts cry in the dark — beasts both of fur and of flesh.
The world beyond the village is no gentle place.

Each fire I build is a battle won.
Each step is taken with care, for the road is lined with those who would sooner take than share.
Yet not all are wicked.
I have met others who walk the same hard path — wanderers, dreamers, those who left safe walls behind in search of something greater.

We do not speak much, but in a glance we understand each other.

I have seen valleys green and deep, rivers swift and silver, forests older than memory.
I have seen hope carried in calloused hands and worn faces.

Though the land seeks to break me, I will not bow.
What little I have is my own.
And though the road is long and full of hardship, it is mine to walk."
